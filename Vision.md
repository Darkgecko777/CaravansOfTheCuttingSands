# Caravans of the Cutting Sands

This document describes the game as it runs. It is a description for grok.com to read. It does not add features.

The title wordmark is the approved logo. The line under it is "Every road has a price."

A new game is House Kharûn and docks at Kharûn with 400 coin and empty packs. Twenty places share one painted map. Profit is the gap between a full shelf and a thin one. Travel is a store of hours. The caravan rests in town or camps on the road when that store runs down to a reserve. Ordinary food in the packs ages. Rations in the packs do not. Other caravans are already buying the same goods.

## The world

Five cities are house seats. The house is named for the city.

- **Kharûn.** Scrubstone, contracts, quiet leverage. Scrubstone comes out of the ground and becomes coin. The city works the lowest grade of shimmersteel into plain jars.
- **Zamath.** Forest, witching rods, pragmatic deals. A shore city of sentient trees. Neth opens from here across the water, and the crossing adds no hours.
- **Thalor.** Proud, formal, dependent on the west. The shimmersteel mine, and the door for Ishka goods from the dunes north of the map.
- **Veythar.** Isolationist, militant, agrarian. Terraced farms. It works other people's shimmersteel into the highest grade.
- **Ghorath.** Debts, secrets, second chances. An oasis fortress. Dream lotus nectar is contraband wherever it is shown.

Nine villages sit off the seats: Ashar, Moraq, Torvern, Draven, Sorel, Kethra, Neth, Rukh, and Ghûl. Each has a market and a plaza. Ghûl shares a road with Ghorath. Six posts sit on the roads: Westmark, Kaleth, Southmark, Highmark, Brineford, and Ridgewatch. Rukh opens from Westmark the way Neth opens from Zamath. Sarn's Rest is a landmark, not a dock.

Shimmersteel is one metal, mined at Thalor. Work changes the finish. Jars are the low grade, cookware the middle, and fineware the high grade. It stands in for bronze, silver, and gold, and it is not coin. Forty-one goods travel the map. Water and rations are made at every settlement, including the posts. A grade at each place sets how full those two shelves open and how fast they refill. An abundant grade opens cheaper. A scarce grade opens dearer. Posts mint no unique good. A new game is one merchant on a thrask. That caravan drinks 10 litres and eats 4 kilograms a day. The kit holds 40 litres and 16 kilograms, and it starts full. The packs hold 4 units and 10 weight, and they start empty. The merchant's thrask carries those packs. A duzhu carries 8 units and 20 weight. A pincer beast carries 16 units and 40 weight. Daily use and kit room also count any hand and any pack beast already on the session. Kharûn's Tavern hires one scout, one haggler, and one guard. Each hired agent rides their own thrask and adds that person's kit share. Play has no screen that buys a mount. Pack room follows only the merchant's mount. Rivals still haul 8 units and 20 weight. Cargo water stays a trade good. Rations do not. The market does not buy or sell them. Fill food still buys one shelf unit into the bag. Ordinary food in the wagon still ages. The kit is what the caravan drinks and eats. Sea water is cargo drawn at Westmark, Neth, Sorel, Zamath, Ghûl, Kaleth, and Brineford. Its buy coin and its sell coin match that place's pure-water buy and sell. A sale adds one to the pure-water shelf and does not stock sea water. Those shores do not buy sea water. Ghorath and Rukh buy neither pure water nor sea water. The caravan does not desalt a unit.

Names and place stories live in `Caravans_Goods.md`. Weight, pack size, base price, and daily appetite live in `Docs/Economy_Weight_Price.csv` and `Docs/Economy_Eat_Per_Day.csv`. The playable game reads those two sheets for every good they list. Water, rations, and sea water are not on them. Water and ration price, weight, and pack are read from `data/config/prices.json`. Sea water uses that same pack, and its price is the pure-water shelf. Grades and the sea-water supply are read from `data/config/abundance.json`. The game does not read `data/lines.json`, `data/markets.json`, or `data/markets.md`. The save is `caravans_save.json` in the player's persistent data folder, `LocalLow\Uncanny Mercantile\Caravans of the Cutting Sands`, with a `.bak` of the previous save. Those files sync through Steam Auto-Cloud configured on the Steamworks site. The game does not call the Steam Cloud API. Volume and intro settings stay in player preferences.

## How a session opens

The title is a desert picture with a bed of blowing sand. The wordmark is the approved logo, and the line under it is "Every road has a price." The title menu is a compact living-lumber wood panel under the tagline, with the desert around it, and text-only items: NEW GAME, OPTIONS, and EXIT. A bone tick marks the selected or checked row. No bar sits behind a row. The game keeps one autosaved run. When it exists, CONTINUE sits above NEW GAME in the same style and is the selected row; it reopens the run at the market of the town where it was saved, without the intro card. NEW GAME over a save asks "Start a new run?" / "This overwrites your saved run." with NEW GAME and BACK. With no save, the menu is NEW GAME, OPTIONS, and EXIT, as before. New Game opens free play as House Kharûn at Kharûn, with 400 coin, empty packs, and a full kit of 40 litres and 16 kilograms, and the title theme fades out over about a second and a half. The first New Game then shows one intro card before any other play control works. The card is that same wood panel, centred over the market. Cream text gives the header "Kharûn, at the edge of the Cutting Sands.", a body about the wagon, the agents, and the purse of scrubstone, the tagline "Every road has a price.", and a text-only BEGIN. Enter, Space, Escape, or BEGIN dismisses it, marks it seen, and turns Show intro again off. Later New Games skip the card unless that row is on. The backdrop does not dismiss it. The clock does not move while it is open, and Escape does not open the pause menu on the frame it closes. Dismissing it leaves the same new game underneath. A title theme loops while the title is up. Under it, six wind clips follow the sand gust: two looping beds and four gusts that overlap at most two at a time. Each clip runs its own hold, noise, and pitch on that gust. The wind fades over 2.2 seconds when the player leaves the title. The theme still fades over about a second and a half. OPTIONS opens master, music, and SFX volume, fullscreen, resolution, Show intro again, and Close, as text rows and slim sliders on that same wood panel, with no bar behind a row. Esc closes Options when it is open. Master sets the listener. Music scales the title theme. SFX scales the title wind and the goods clicks. Headers and menu words are Cutting Sands. The rest of the text is Bona Nova SC.

In the Editor, TRAVEL DEMO sits apart from that wood panel. It opens a side-on sandbox and does not start a run. On a Dune Sea road the sandbox scrolls that terrain's sky, distant plain, dune lines, and ground strips on one shared sand line, with a sand fill behind the dunes. The land fills about three fifths of the frame. Near strips are one seeded copy of that road's sand, butt-joined. Scrub and stone stand in that near sand, at uneven gaps, sometimes in a cluster of two or three. A piece set lower in the sand is slightly larger. They keep the near pace, and their bases stay under the crest. The starter thrask stands in that sand with its feet under the crest, and stands still at Camp. The supply panel sits clear below the crest. Other terrains stay placeholder shapes. Back returns to the title. Live travel is still the painted map.

A run lasts until the player returns to the title, quits, or leaves play mode. Esc, or Options on the top bar, opens a pause menu on that compact wood panel: RESUME, OPTIONS, MAIN MENU, and QUIT. A bone tick marks the selected row. No bar sits behind a row. The clock stays frozen while the pause menu or its Options sheet is open. OPTIONS uses the same sheet as the title, including Show intro again. Close on that sheet returns to the pause list. MAIN MENU returns to the title. QUIT leaves the game. Options stays visible while ShowUnfinishedFeatures is off. The stables and the house room stay off the dock. At Kharûn the tavern is on the bar. It sells the six pieces of Sarn's map, and it hires one scout, one haggler, and one guard. At every other dock the tavern stays off.

The editor menu Caravans/Capture UI Screenshots writes one 1920×1080 picture of each screen into `Screenshots/` at the project root and lists them in `Screenshots/index.md`. The editor menu Caravans/Build Windows writes a Windows x64 player to `Builds/Windows/`. Steamworks initializes when Steam is running and leaves play alone when it is not. Four achievements report through the local unlock helper: buying the first piece of Sarn's map, showing the Embarked ledger, reaching House Kharûn rank 5, and the first player flip of a village or post. A continue keeps those local flags. A new game clears them. Steam keeps an unlock that already happened.

A buy spends coin and takes a unit off the shelf onto the next open slot, if the purse can pay, the shelf has stock, and the wagon has a free slot and weight left. Each unit takes one slot. The pack column on the weight sheet does not set how a unit fits. The starting weight cap is 10, so two units of weight 5 fill it and a third does not. Lighter goods fill the four slots first. Buy coin is the shelf after that unit leaves, times one plus the merchant spread, rounded, at least one coin, then any cut. Sell coin is the shelf after that unit returns, times the unit's remaining integrity and one minus the spread, rounded, then any cut. The spread in the file is 15 percent. A hired haggler makes it 10 percent each way. Standing does not change it. Buying and selling in the same market does not raise the purse. Profit is the gap between towns, and between a full shelf and a thin one. Selling at a village or a post adds House Kharûn to the local influence. A unit bought in that place on this visit and sold back before departure adds none. Cargo from another town still does. The house with the most influence holds the place. Each morning that influence falls to three quarters. A rival holder takes a flat cut of 12 percent. Rank does not change that cut. House Kharûn, and a place with no patron, take no cut. The merchant is House Kharûn. Standing exists only with House Kharûn. It rises from profit on each sale, the coin received after the cut minus what that unit cost, and from 15 points the first time a player sale flips each village or post to House Kharûn. There are 15 such places. A loss, a break-even sale, a same-visit resale, a kit pour, rations, and goods from a card or a find add no sale points. One hundred points make a rank. The first rank takes 18 coin of profit per point, and each rank already held adds 8 coin to that. The cap is rank 10, and a reached rank never drops. There is no session save yet, so the points, the rank, and those first-flip marks live on the session.

A shortfall past the supply names how many skins and bundles to haul. Back leaves the road lit. Take the road anyway leaves. A sea opening leaves at once. The lit trail stays until the wagon arrives. On the road the same painting is zoomed so the line of sight is as tall as the view, with the wagon at the center. A wide frame shows fog past the circle at the sides. Near the edge of the painting the wagon stays centered and the frame shows the empty margin. The player's caravan is a static mount token on the painted stroke, and it docks at the center of the place. The travel map and the Gates map mark the player's caravan and NPC merchants with static mount tokens. The player token follows the merchant's mount (thrask at new game, then duzhu, then pincer beast, including after Continue). NPC merchants always ride a duzhu told apart by pack colour. There is no glow and no animation. Nine of those merchants use the same strokes. The radius is wider at noon, tighter at night, and tightest inside a sandstorm. The map itself cools toward night. The travel store is hours. A new game starts at 12. Travel stops with 3 hours left unspent. Those 3 hours stay unspent, and the screen still shows hours. Nine hours of the full store can be spent before that stop. Waiting an hour spends one of them, at one twenty-fourth of this caravan's day. A road spends them as the caravan moves. On the road, in camp, and while waiting, a skin pours into the kit when the water there is down to 12 hours of road use, counted at the day rate even at night, and a ration bundle opens the same way. A dock does not pour. The pour is the whole unit, 25 litres or 10 kilograms, and it waits when the kit has no room for that unit. Sea water never pours. When the water or the rations on hand, kit and packs together, are down to 12 hours of that road use, the supply line turns amber and the leg line reads The skins run low. At 6 hours the line turns red and, once on that leg, the road pauses on a card titled The Skins Run Low. At a dock the line can turn red and that card does not open. The roads do not go grey because the skins are short. Rest and camp spend none of them. Each refills the store to 12.

A neighbor road lights its trail. Careful, Steady, and Rush sit as three compact plates in a panel under the roads. Steady starts pressed, and choosing another road keeps the pace. Once a road is lit, the panel shows the day hours in gold and the night hours in blue until the next stop. Embark confirms the departure and is grey until a road is lit. The same choice is asked again after a camp. When a scout is hired, that card and the Gates panel show the encounter chance for the pressed pace: 35 percent Careful, 50 percent Steady, and 65 percent Rush. The line is hidden when no scout is hired or no road is lit. Take the road continues the leg when the skins cover it. When they do not, the warning card offers Take the road anyway or Back, and Back leaves the pace card up. Take the road keeps the ordinary button color. Under the words it shows the day hours in gold and the night hours in blue until the caravan next stops. A stop is the forced camp, when the spendable travel hours run out, or arrival, when the leg is shorter. Day is hour 6 up to hour 18. A sea opening shows no hour line. Steady is the ordinary pace. Its speed is 1 and its kit factor is 1. Rush speed is 1.5, so the same road takes two thirds of the Steady hours, and each of those hours drinks 1.25 of the ordinary kit. Careful speed is 0.5, so the road takes twice the Steady hours, and each hour drinks 0.75 of the ordinary kit. On the road, an hour from 6 up to 18 also multiplies water and rations by 1.5, on top of the pace. Night hours on the road use the pace kit alone. The pace does not change those clock hours on a long leg, because the camp still comes after the same spend. On a short leg the pace can decide whether the caravan arrives or camps, and the hour line follows that. Wait, rest, and camp use a factor of 1.

Each leg rolls once for an encounter when the wagon leaves, at the pace chosen for that departure. Careful is 35 percent, Steady is 50 percent, and Rush is 65 percent. A hit stores a whole hour from 1 through 6 and does not lock a card yet. The wagon travels that many hours of the leg, then pauses and picks a written card. Day is hour 6 up to hour 18, and the card's day or night is read at that hour. A card listed as either can open at either hour. The pick also reads the road and any flag the card requires or blocks. Only cards in the road pool are drawn. Catch-all and bandits are written for every road. Kulva's Warning is written for the roads that meet Zamath. Rival-house rows stay in the chart and off the road until a later pass can tell that the leg crossed a caravan of a house. BA-04 and BA-05 stay in the chart and are never drawn. If the leg arrives before the hour, the card does not open. The card shows its title, a scene of about 200 characters, and two choices. The id stays off the card. A choice applies that line, then the card shows the result and Continue. Continue sends the wagon on. A card can change coin, cargo, the kit, or the clock. It cannot drain a store below six hours of supply at the pace in use. Supply is the kit plus skins and bundles in the packs. The card takes from the kit only. A hired scout or guard satisfies that skill on a card. Handler and Gossip stay unmet. The Friend of Bandits trait is still only a test hook. The next departure on Kaleth–Zamath, Zamath–Southmark, Kharûn–Kaleth, Southmark–Sorel, or Southmark–Ghûl opens The God Under the Water in place of that leg's roll. The same road, in the same weather, at the same pace, takes the same travel hours. Heat and wind stretch a leg by about a sixth. A sandstorm stretches it by two fifths. ## The market the world runs

A new run begins on 1 Vekmar, Year 112, at hour 6, which is Dawn. The year is 240 days: Vekmar, Dravun, Morvek, Thulor, Zarneth, Kalvorn, Sorvek, Torvak, thirty days each, counting from Year 112. The day is eight parts of three hours: Dawn, Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Dusk, Evening, Night, and Deep night. The top bar and the logbook read like 1 Vekmar, Year 112 · Dawn. Morning and night for the kit are still hours 6 and 18. Midnight rolls the day. Waiting an hour, waiting until dawn or dusk, time on the road, a town rest, and a camp all cross those hours.

Each morning, every place eats from its shelves by the appetite sheet. Stock that hits zero stops being eaten until more arrives. Ordinary food in the packs loses a tenth of its integrity. Sweet Bliss loses a quarter. A unit near zero leaves the packs. Water does not age. Rations in the packs do not age. A unit bought on a morning does not age again that same morning. Sale coin follows the worst unit of that good still aboard.

Each night, a place that produces a good makes more of it, up to a cap. The nightly amount scales with `productionScale` in `data/config/prices.json`, which is 1. Water and rations use that scale on the local grade instead of the shared make. Sea water is made only on the shore list, from the supply in `data/config/abundance.json`. Five workshops craft from stock on hand. The place, the goods, and the input counts are read from `data/config/workshops.json`:

- Kharûn turns shimmersteel into jars.
- Thalor turns shimmersteel and Titan's Heart-rock into cookware.
- Veythar turns shimmersteel into fineware.
- Zamath turns living lumber into water witching rods.
- Ghûl turns numbspindle into hulv bone.

A shelf's coin comes from how many days of local appetite the stock covers. Those bands are read from `data/config/prices.json`. An empty shelf is double the base price. Three days of cover is the base. Six days is half. Buy coin sits above the shelf after the unit leaves, by the merchant spread, then any cut. The spread is 15 percent, or 10 percent each way while a haggler is hired. Sell coin sits below the shelf after the unit returns, by that same spread, times remaining integrity, then any cut. A same-market round trip cannot make coin. The figure on a selected shop cell is the buy coin, and an empty shelf still shows it. The figure on a selected wagon unit is the sell coin, when the place buys the good. Selecting the cell records the figure that the strip is showing. An empty shelf's price is shown and is not stored. A buy figure is stored only when that shelf has stock. A sale figure is stored only for a wagon unit whose price is on the strip. Nothing is stored while the caravan is on the road, while a camp is pending, or while a pace is being chosen.

Nine rival caravans share the map: one of Kharûn and two of each other house. They buy a profitable load inside eight slots and a weight cap of 20, travel at most two hops, and sell on arrival. That profit uses the same merchant spread. A sale at a village or a post can move who holds the place. Each unit of that sale is priced on the shelf after the unit is added. They trade everywhere. Their tokens are on the map: each rides a static duzhu told apart by pack colour. The player has no screen for their cargo.

Starting stock is thinner the more hops a place sits from a producer. A place six or more hops out starts empty of that good. Caps sit at six days of local appetite, or at a night's make, whichever is larger. Water and rations do not use that hop gradient. Their opening cover and their nightly make come from the local grade in `data/config/abundance.json`. A is six days and one and a half times the local appetite. B is four days and 1.15. C is three days and 0.85. D is one day and 0.4. The shelf price is still the cover curve. Sea water starts only on the shores that draw it. Elsewhere it starts empty, and a sale never adds to a sea-water pile.

## Tunable numbers

Play reads `data/config/` from disk when a session needs a rate. The files hold the numbers the running game already uses. The same read reaches `Docs/Economy_Weight_Price.csv`, `Docs/Economy_Eat_Per_Day.csv`, `Encounter_IDs.md`, and `Route_Tags.md`. A player build copies those files into the folder that contains the exe, which is where the player looks.

`consumption.json` holds the morning integrity loss: a tenth for ordinary food, a quarter for Sweet Bliss. It holds the daily ration appetite of 1, which the eat sheet does not list. It also holds a fallback kit of 25 litres and 10 kilograms a day, 75 litres, and 30 kilograms, used only when `kit_consumption.json` is missing, and a shelf unit of 25 litres or 10 kilograms. The running kit is `kit_consumption.json`. A new game uses the thrask: 10 litres and 4 kilograms a day, a kit of 40 litres and 16 kilograms, and packs of 4 units and 10 weight. That file also holds the duzhu and the pincer beast, the pour at 12 hours, and the warning at 12 hours and 6 hours.

`prices.json` holds the shelf bands, the merchant spread of 15 percent, the nightly production scale, a flat rival cut of 12 percent, and Kharûn standing: a cap of rank 10, 100 points a rank, 18 coin of profit per point plus 8 for each rank already held, and 15 points for a first player flip. It also holds the morning keep of three quarters of village and post influence, and the water and ration price, weight, and pack. `agents.json` holds the tavern hires: scout 40 and 6, haggler 80 and 12, guard 60 and 8, a haggler spread of 0.10, and a Rush cargo-loss chance of 1.

`weather.json` holds the leg stretches. Heat and wind multiply a leg by 1.15. A sandstorm multiplies it by 1.4. A clear leg stays at 1, and that 1 is not a field in the file. The same file holds the vision radii: 0.17 by day, 0.09 at night, and 0.07 inside a sandstorm. `weather_look.json` holds the grade and haze colours, their alphas, the fade time and the patch look, and changes nothing in play. Cargo loss is not in the weather file. On Rush, a bad encounter can take a pack unit. That chance is in `agents.json`.

`travel.json` holds the starting 400 coin, the fallback pack limits of 8 slots and 20 weight, which rivals still use, and the clock. Morning is hour 6, night is hour 18, and a day is 24 hours. A clear Steady day of road at 1× takes 36 real seconds while the wagon is moving. A sea opening still adds no hours on the player's clock. Rivals treat that opening as one hour when they score a trip, from `seaHourFloor`. The same file holds the travel store of 12 hours, the reserve of 3 hours, a town rest of 4 hours, a camp of 6 hours, and the three paces: Steady speed 1 and kit 1, Rush speed 1.5 and kit 1.25, Careful speed 0.5 and kit 0.75. It also holds `dayKit` at 1.5, the extra water and ration rate for a day hour on the road.

`soft_reset.json` holds the waking kit of one day, the carried coin kept at half, the floor of 40 coin, and the return to Kharûn. `rescue_texts.json` holds the Dry title, the lead, and one set of lines and a day count for each region.

`rivals.json` holds the two-hop limit. Each new game draws its own seed, which places the nine rival caravans and drives encounter rolls, and Continue resumes that run's draws.

`workshops.json` holds the five recipes named above. It names places and goods by id, such as Kharun and ShimmersteelJars.

`abundance.json` holds the four grades and one row per place, plus the sea-water supply: shores start with 4, make 2 a night, and cap at 12. The grade letters are the locked table. A later tune changes the day counts and the make rates in that file.

`encounters.json` holds the leg chances: a base of 0.5, a careful offset of -0.15, a rush offset of 0.15, and a floor of 0.05. Those three paces land on 35, 50, and 65 percent. The floor does not change them. A hit opens its card after a whole hour from `fireHourMin` 1 through `fireHourMax` 6. The written cards, their scenes, and their outcomes are `encounter_cards.json` in the same folder. Upgrade costs have no file yet.

## The logbook

Logbook on the top bar opens a parchment chart of every good, in catalog order, including water, rations, and sea water. The sheet sits between the top and bottom bars with a 24-point margin, so the tabs stay visible. The columns are Good, Lowest buy, and Highest sale. They are sized to the words and centred as one block. Row text is 24 points in dark ink. The header carries the same calendar line as the top bar. Each row shows that good's picture in a goods cell when the file loads, and the cell with the name when it does not. Each row keeps the lowest buy and the highest sale the market strip has shown for that good, and the settlement of each figure. A buy is stored when a selected shop cell has stock. A sale is stored when a selected wagon unit's price is on the strip. An empty shelf's price is shown and is not stored. An unseen price is a soft dash. A later cheaper buy replaces the low. A later higher sale replaces the high. Talk, rumours, and leaving someone to watch a market do not write a row. Above the goods, a short Sarn's map block lists each piece held and the day it was bought. With none held it reads "No pieces of Sarn's map yet." Under that block, Road notes lists each Rush loss, newest first. With none it reads "No road notes yet." Banked leads show their count. The block has no Buy.

## Sarn's map

All six pieces of Sarn's map are sold at Kharûn's Tavern, in order from the first to the sixth. House Kharûn rank opens a piece. The ranks are 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, and 10. The list prices are 600, 1900, 3800, 5800, 8000, and 10400 coin. Rank does not discount the price. A banked lead takes 10 percent off the next piece, and a second lead takes another 10 percent. At most two leads sit at once. The purchase spends every banked lead, then the bank is empty. The next piece starts again at its list price. Each row reads "A fragment of Sarn's map." A locked row reads "Needs Kharûn rank N" and has no Buy. Buy is grey only when the purse is short.

A piece is a key item. It takes no cargo slot and no weight. It is never sold and never stolen. The logbook lists the ones you hold, with the day each was bought. A desert death keeps the pieces, the purchase days, the banked leads, the coin paid, the leads already followed, the journey, the ledger tallies, and the four achievement flags. A new game clears them. The autosave keeps them with the run, and the Embarked ending clears that save.

Owning all six opens a road from Westmark's Gates to Sarn's Rest. It is not a dock and not a neighbour on the painted roads. The gate is 12 Steady hours, and the road back is the same. Encounters can fire on both. The arrival card is titled Sarn's Rest. Its choices are Embark and Return later. Embark starts an 18 Steady-hour run with no road back and no random encounters. Three fixed cards sit along that run. The run ends on a ledger titled Embarked. One button, Return to the title, leaves play. The first piece bought marks that achievement. Showing the ledger marks the second. Reaching House Kharûn rank 5 marks the third. The first flip of a village or post to House Kharûn marks the fourth.

## Tavern agents

Kharûn's Tavern hires one scout, one haggler, and one guard. Hire costs are 40, 80, and 60 coin. Dawn wages are 6, 12, and 8 coin, paid at the start of each morning in that order. The first wage is the next dawn after hire, including a dawn on the road or at the inn. If the purse cannot cover an agent's wage, that agent leaves at once, the coin stays at or above zero, and a short toast names who left. Dismiss at Kharûn returns no coin. There is no rank gate. Hire and dismiss are refused at any other dock and while the caravan is on the road. Each hired agent rides their own thrask and adds that person's kit share. The roles and the road notes survive a desert death. A new game clears them. The autosave keeps the roles and the road notes with the run.

A hired scout shows the encounter chance on the Gates panel and on the pace card after camp: 35 percent at Careful, 50 percent at Steady, and 65 percent at Rush. The line is hidden when no scout is hired or no road is lit. A hired haggler cuts the merchant spread from 15 percent to 10 percent on both the buy and the sale. Standing does not change the spread. A hired guard turns the first bad encounter of a leg into the line "Your guard handled it." and applies none of that outcome. The save returns at the start of the next leg, including the Westmark gate, the road back, and the final run. Camp in the middle of a road does not restore it. That save also blocks the Rush cargo loss on the outcome it cleans.

On Rush, a bad encounter that still applies removes one random unit from the packs, not from the kit, and not coin when the packs are empty. The result names the good. The logbook keeps the dated line, newest first. Careful and Steady leave the packs whole. Weather does not take cargo. The chance is in `agents.json` and starts at 1, so a bad Rush outcome takes a unit whenever the packs hold one.
